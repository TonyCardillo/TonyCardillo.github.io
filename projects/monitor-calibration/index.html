Quick Monitor Check

4 quick tests to make sure your screen
is ready for digital sign-out.

Takes about 1 minute.

Created by Anthony Cardillo, MD
Step 1 of 4

Brightness & Contrast

Can you see every stripe below as a separate shade?
Pay close attention to the very darkest and very lightest ones.

The dark or light end of the strip is blending together.
How to fix:
  1. Look for a physical button or joystick on the bottom or side edge of your monitor
  2. Open the on-screen menu and find Brightness — set it to about 75–80%
  3. Find Contrast — set it to about 70–80%
  4. If the dark stripes merge: raise Brightness
  5. If the light stripes merge: lower Brightness or raise Contrast
Step 2 of 4

Subtle Differences

Each gray square below has a circle inside it.
Can you see all four circles?

You may miss subtle findings on slides.
How to fix:
  1. On your monitor's on-screen menu, increase Contrast to 80–90%
  2. If available, set the display Gamma to 2.2
  3. Make sure no "Eco" or "Dynamic Contrast" mode is turned on — these reduce contrast
  4. If the dark-background circles are hardest to see, try raising Brightness slightly

Color Discrimination

Can you see the circle in every square?

Hematoxylin
Eosin
DAB
PAS
Trichrome
Your monitor may not be rendering subtle color differences accurately. Each pair differs by 3 ΔE00 (CIEDE2000, per POUQA methodology).
How to fix:
  1. On your monitor's on-screen menu, look for Color Temperature or Color Mode
  2. Set it to sRGB if available, or 6500K (sometimes labeled "Normal" or "Standard")
  3. Turn off any "Vivid", "Dynamic", or "Eco" color mode — use Standard
  4. Make sure Saturation is near its default (usually 50%)
  5. If colors still look identical, right-click your desktop → Display Settings → check that Color Profile is set to sRGB
Step 4 of 4

Sharpness

Look at the black and white lines below.
Can you see them as separate, crisp lines — not blurred or merged together?

Text and cell details may look fuzzy.
How to fix:
  1. Right-click your desktop → Display Settings
  2. Set Resolution to the highest option (your monitor's native resolution)
  3. Set Scale to 100% (if text is too small at 100%, try 125% — but 100% is sharpest)
  4. If using a dock or adapter, make sure the cable supports your full resolution (use DisplayPort or the cable that came with the monitor)
  5. On your monitor menu, turn off any Sharpness enhancement (set to 0 or default)
